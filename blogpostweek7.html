Week 7 Response

The first two chapters of The Electronic Disturbance make a really good argument about why electronic resistance is essential in our current times. As they mention, power has moved from having physical and tangible manifestations into intangible, virtual ones. Before, you could occupy spaces and disrupt the elites and their interests in that way like they did during the French revolution. Nowadays, the elites and their assets are hard to pin down. A good example of this is how in the past cash used to be the main way people would exchange money and pay for things and now we have stores where they don’t take cash at all. How can there be redistribution of wealth when wealth isn’t tangible? This is why it’s important to not only resist in the physical world but also in the digital realm.

However, resisting in the digital realm is so much harder than in the physical world. Learning how to hack an electronic system requires a lot of advanced programming knowledge that most people don’t have and the authors even acknowledge that the few people who could do this aren’t politicized at all. On top of that, the article was published in 1994 and the authors argue that “The electronic world, however, is by no means fully established, and it is time to take advantage of this fluidity through invention, before we are left with only critique as a weapon”. Now, in 2026, I’d say that unfortunately the digital world has been fully established and controlled by tech corporations so I’m left with a big question. How do we perform electronic resistance in an age where power has been consolidated by tech oligarchs?
