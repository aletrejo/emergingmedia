Week 1 Responses

While reading “In Defense of the Poor Image” I couldn’t help but think about how drastically the internet has changed since 2009, the year the article was published. Steyerl talks about poor images that have bad quality and low resolutions but nowadays you’d be hard pressed to find a low resolution video even if it’s a video recorded with a phone by an amateur. I checked and it wasn’t until November 2009, the same month the article came out, that 1080p resolution was introduced to Youtube and the average broadband speed at that time was around 8 Mbps. If anything, low resolution is almost an aesthetic choice today.

And yet, aside from the technological advancements, there are claims from the article that are still true and relevant in 2026. Like the fact that experimental and independent cinema has been relegated to a second tier. In that regard, those are still poor images. But, in 2026, are there other new kinds of poor images? My mind immediately jumps to AI generated images. In the final paragraph of the article Steyerl states that “the poor image is no longer about the real thing – the originary original”. I believe this applies to images created by AI but it feels wrong to lump these images with the countercultural artifacts shared clandestinely by film aficionados. Is AI slop a more appropriate term? This reading left me with a lot to think about.
