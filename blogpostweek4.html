Week 4 Responses

It’s true that history repeats itself. That’s what I felt reading Claire Bishop’s Artificial Hells. Specifically, the section on Italian Futurists' use of participatory art to push forward militant nationalism. Bishop claims that “Futurist approach to participation was via negativa – as a form of total emotional response in which one could not occupy the position of a distanced observer but was incited to take part in an orgy of destruction”. I couldn't help but think of the current state of discourse in the present times, especially online, and how that’s degraded so much to the point that it feels as if there’s no longer a space for people to have meaningful debates.

While Italian futurists used the theater as the space to encounter audiences, I would say that the modern equivalent would be social media. When social media first started out, it was deemed as a space where everyone could have a voice and share their opinions and engage in conversations with each other. Of course, now we know that that’s not what happened. Bishop describes the results of Italian futurists serate “a reduction to mob mentality, and the abandonment of critical distance and reasoned logic to a mindless anticipation of the future through nationalist violence”. You could use that same sentence to describe the social media users of today. People on social media aren’t interested in meaningful debate, they just want to dunk on each other. The winner is oftentimes not the person with the most solid argument, but the one who gets the most likes, the one that ratios the other, that owns them. The space might have changed from a physical theater to a non-physical digital realm but the underlying principles and violence that fueled Futurist’s serates and today’s social media is the exact same.
